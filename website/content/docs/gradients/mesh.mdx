A mesh places one color at each point of a `rows × columns` grid and fills the space between them with smooth bicubic patches. It's the signature look of modern wallpapers and onboarding screens.

`colors` lists one color per point, row by row. With fewer colors than points, the list repeats.

```tsx
<MeshGradient
  rows={2}
  columns={2}
  colors={['#FF6B6B', '#FFD166', '#4D96FF', '#6BCB77']}
/>
```

## Moving the points

`points` repositions the control points explicitly, in unit coordinates and in the same order as `colors`. There must be exactly `rows × columns` of them. Moving the inner points pulls colors into organic shapes; with a [transition](/docs/guides/transitions), they glide.

`drift` sets them wandering on their own. Edge points only slide along their edge, so the mesh always covers the view.

## Smoothness

`smoothness` blends between straight bilinear patches (`0`) and a spline that curves through every point (`1`, the default).

<Callout type="tip" title="Cost">
  A still mesh is tessellated once and cached. A drifting mesh re-tessellates on the CPU every frame, so keep it to one or two on screen. See [Performance](/docs/guides/performance#recommendations).
</Callout>
